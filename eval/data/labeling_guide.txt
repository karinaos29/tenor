- urgency: Pushes a decision now: a deadline today or within hours, a price 'locked' until a time, 'decide before we hang up'. NOT this: A real, distant deadline stated calmly with no push (e.g. a tax-year date, 'no rush').
- scarcity: Says the opportunity is rare, exclusive or limited: only a few places, a select group of clients, 'not offered to the public'. NOT this: Plain product facts such as 'this account has no minimum'.
- social_proof: Uses what other people supposedly do or buy to push the listener: 'most of my clients take 10,000', 'thousands have already joined'. NOT this: Neutral information without a push, e.g. 'some customers start with a monthly amount, then adjust'.
- secrecy: Asks the listener not to tell or consult family, friends, the bank or an adviser, or to keep the deal quiet. NOT this: Normal privacy, e.g. 'I can't discuss your account with anyone else'.
- guarantee: Promises returns or says there is no risk: 'guaranteed', 'risk-free', 'can't lose', 'a sure thing', 'it will double'. NOT this: A legal deposit protection stated factually (e.g. 'deposits are insured up to the legal limit'), or 'past returns don't guarantee future results'.
- fomo_regret: Plays on regret or missing out: 'people who waited regretted it', 'don't miss out', 'you'll kick yourself'. NOT this: Neutral history without pressure, e.g. 'markets have gone up and down over time'.
- skip_checks: Discourages paperwork, written details or verification: 'no time for paperwork', 'I'll confirm by phone', 'you don't need to read it'. NOT this: Offering to send documents or saying 'take your time to read it'.
- authority: Leans on vague experts, insiders or famous names to persuade: 'our analysts say', 'insiders know', 'it's the next Apple', 'endorsed by …'. NOT this: Naming a specific, checkable source of a fact (e.g. 'the fund's factsheet lists the fee').
